# 📊 DAS1 — Data Analyst Senior I

*Autonomous analytics contributor and business-facing problem solver*

You independently turn ambiguous business questions into trusted, decision-ready analytics. You combine strong SQL, modeling, visualization, and storytelling with stakeholder discovery, data-quality judgment, and responsible AI-enabled execution.

> Source: [DAS1 - Data Analyst Senior I (2026 version)](https://qubika.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/QCPF/pages/5420449813/DAS1+-+Data+Analyst+Senior+I+2026+version) — Qubika's Career Path Framework (QCPF), last modified Jul 28, 2026.

## 👤 Role Identity

- Owns analytics products from business question and metric definition through delivery, adoption, and iteration.
- Acts as a trusted project-level partner who clarifies needs and makes data limitations and trade-offs explicit.
- Builds understandable, maintainable, governed, and reusable analytical assets rather than one-off reports.
- Uses AI to accelerate analysis and development while preserving correctness, traceability, and human judgment.

## 🔍 Role at a Glance

| **Dimension** | **Expected scope** |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary scope** | A dashboard, metric set, analytical model, report, or bounded decision-support workstream. |
| **Autonomy** | Works independently in familiar and moderately ambiguous analytics situations; aligns on high-impact definitions and decisions. |
| **Complexity** | Solves questions involving multiple data sources, unclear requirements, conflicting metric definitions, or adoption challenges. |
| **Client relationship** | Facilitates discovery, challenges requests constructively, and translates business needs into trusted analytical outcomes. |
| **Influence** | Improves the immediate analytics team and contributes reusable knowledge, standards, or assets. |
| **Outcome standard** | Decision-ready, understandable, validated, maintainable, secure, and adopted analytics. |

## 📈 Growth Zone

At this level, your impact shifts from producing reports to owning trusted analytics outcomes. You should increasingly understand the decision behind the request, create reusable foundations, and help the customer move from data to action.

### ▶️ Forward-deployed direction

Your forward-deployed growth path is to become an embedded decision partner: understand the business workflow, define shared outcomes, rapidly build useful analytics, integrate them into how decisions are made, and measure adoption rather than stopping at dashboard delivery.

## 🤝 1. Teamwork

Contributes to collective success and increases the autonomy and effectiveness of teammates across analytics, engineering, product, and client teams.

**What is expected**
- Shares context, assumptions, and decisions so teammates can move independently.
- Reviews and pairs constructively, explaining reasoning rather than only correcting the output.
- Supports onboarding and the growth of less-experienced teammates without becoming a permanent dependency.
- Raises delivery, quality, ownership, or team-health risks early and helps drive them toward resolution.
- Works respectfully across cultures, disciplines, time zones, and different levels of technical depth.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A teammate delivered independently after coaching or knowledge transfer.
- A blocker, ownership gap, or cross-functional misunderstanding was resolved through their action.
- Peer feedback recognizes reliability, useful reviews, or the ability to make collaboration easier.

**Growth direction**
Progress from helping individuals to intentionally shaping team ownership, accountability, and collaboration practices.

## 💬 2. Communication

Communicates analytics decisions, uncertainty, progress, and value with clarity, intent, and audience awareness.

**What is expected**
- Explains decisions, rationale, alternatives, limitations, and trade-offs.
- Adapts language and depth for technical teams, business stakeholders, managers, and clients.
- Communicates progress, dependencies, risks, and decisions proactively rather than waiting to be asked.
- Produces concise documentation that supports delivery, adoption, operation, and future ownership.
- Connects the analysis, metric, dashboard, report, or decision-support product to the customer problem, the decision it enables, and the outcome it is intended to improve.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A decision record, workshop, demo, presentation, or status update that enabled alignment.
- Ambiguity or stakeholder misalignment was clarified before it caused avoidable rework.
- Stakeholder feedback recognizes clarity, credibility, responsiveness, or useful challenge.

**Growth direction**
Progress from clear project communication to leading difficult alignment and decision-making across diverse stakeholders.

## 🌱 3. Continuous Improvement

Owns professional growth and converts learning in analytics, domain knowledge, consulting, and AI-enabled work into stronger execution.

**What is expected**
- Maintains a focused growth plan aligned with project needs, Studio direction, and identified gaps.
- Applies lessons from feedback, retrospectives, incidents, reviews, and unsuccessful approaches.
- Pursues relevant learning or certifications and demonstrates practical application.
- Experiments with new tools or techniques using a clear problem, hypothesis, constraints, and success criteria.
- Shares validated lessons so the team benefits from individual learning.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A changed behavior or expanded responsibility tied to a development objective.
- A course, certification, paper, or experiment translated into production or delivery value.
- A repeated inefficiency improved after feedback, a retrospective, or an incident.

**Growth direction**
Move from improving personal capability to systematically developing others and scaling lessons across projects.

## 📦 4. Delivery

Owns complete analytics outcomes from business question and metric definition through trusted analysis, visualization, adoption, and follow-through.

**What is expected**
- Clarifies the business question, decision, target audience, success measure, and delivery constraints before building.
- Profiles and validates source data and makes data limitations explicit.
- Defines metrics and business rules consistently and aligns them with relevant owners.
- Builds maintainable analytical models, dashboards, reports, or self-service assets with appropriate testing and documentation.
- Applies information design, accessibility, and storytelling principles so the output supports a decision rather than displaying data only.
- Measures adoption or usefulness and iterates based on stakeholder feedback.
- Owns data-quality, interpretation, or delivery issues through resolution.

**What good evidence looks like**
- An analytics product delivered independently from discovery through adoption.
- A metric definition, analysis, or dashboard changed a real business or operational decision.
- A data-quality or interpretation risk was identified and resolved before it damaged trust.

**Growth direction**
Expand from owning individual analytics products to owning a coherent analytics domain, semantic model, and decision roadmap.

## 🏆 5. Leadership

Leads through ownership, judgment, example, and the ability to improve the performance of others, regardless of formal title.

**What is expected**
- Provides direction when clarity is needed and invites relevant perspectives before deciding.
- Delegates meaningful work with context, decision boundaries, and appropriate follow-up.
- Gives timely, specific, and actionable feedback and receives feedback without defensiveness.
- Maintains a constructive environment during pressure, setbacks, or changing priorities.
- Acknowledges mistakes, corrects course, and communicates the learning.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A teammate took greater ownership after coaching or delegation.
- A difficult situation was handled with clear decisions and constructive follow-through.
- Feedback or role-modeling led to an observable improvement in another person or the team.

**Growth direction**
Progress toward leading complex delivery through others and representing the Studio in high-stakes situations.

## 🏗️ 6. Studio / Company Impact

Contributes beyond immediate assignments in ways that strengthen Qubika's analytics, talent, delivery capability, or market credibility.

**What is expected**
- Understands relevant Studio priorities and chooses a focused contribution aligned with them.
- Shares validated project knowledge through sessions, documentation, interviews, case studies, or reusable assets.
- Supports hiring, onboarding, mentoring, certifications, communities of practice, or partner initiatives when relevant.
- Represents Qubika professionally and communicates the value of the team's work.
- Balances internal contribution with reliable client and project commitments.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A reusable playbook, template, code asset, case study, technical session, or evaluation rubric.
- Calibrated interview feedback, meaningful onboarding support, or mentoring impact.
- Contribution to a strategic capability such as Databricks, analytics, data science, ML/AI, governance, or AI adoption.

**Growth direction**
Move from contributing to initiatives to shaping capabilities, standards, talent, and strategic outcomes.

## 🔧 7. Technical Expertise / Mastery

Builds trusted, understandable, performant, and maintainable analytics products using strong SQL, data modeling, visualization, and business-analysis practices.

**What is expected**
- Writes clear, performant SQL and can diagnose common logic, join, aggregation, and data-quality issues.
- Designs analytical and dimensional models that support understandable and reusable metrics.
- Builds effective dashboards and reports in a primary BI platform using sound information design, UX, and accessibility practices.
- Understands warehouse or lakehouse fundamentals, transformations, lineage, refresh patterns, access controls, and basic performance considerations.
- Documents metric definitions, business logic, source lineage, assumptions, ownership, and known limitations.
- Uses version control, review, testing, and deployment practices appropriate to the analytics stack.
- Can use Python, notebooks, dbt, or equivalent tools when they materially improve analysis, automation, or maintainability.

**What good evidence looks like**
- A semantic or analytical model reused by multiple reports or stakeholders.
- A complex SQL, data-quality, performance, or metric inconsistency diagnosed systematically.
- A dashboard or analytics experience improved through evidence-based design and user feedback.

**Growth direction**
Grow from strong analytics product delivery to owning semantic architecture, KPI governance, performance, and analytics standards across a domain.

## 🤖 8. AI Adoption

Integrates AI into real work to improve effectiveness and delivery while maintaining accountability, security, reproducibility, and critical judgment.

**What is expected**
- Uses approved AI tools consistently across relevant analysis, implementation, testing, documentation, troubleshooting, or research activities.
- Provides sufficient context, constraints, and examples rather than relying on generic prompting.
- Validates generated code, queries, calculations, conclusions, and documentation before use.
- Protects client and company information and follows security, confidentiality, and licensing expectations.
- Demonstrates a concrete improvement in cycle time, quality, coverage, analysis depth, or decision-making.
- Uses AI to accelerate SQL development, profiling, documentation, dashboard prototyping, and insight exploration while independently validating data, calculations, definitions, and narratives.
- Shares practical lessons, including limitations and cases where AI should not be used.

**What good evidence looks like**
- Before-and-after evidence of reduced effort, better quality, faster diagnosis, or fewer review iterations.
- A repeatable AI-assisted workflow, prompt, skill, template, or automation used by teammates.
- An example where AI output was rejected or corrected through sound professional judgment.

**Growth direction**
Advance from personal and team-level acceleration to defining scalable, governed AI practices across projects or the Studio.

## ⭐ Rating Reminder

A **3 - Meets expectations** means consistently performing at this seniority. A 4 or 5 requires sustained evidence of meaningful next-level scope and impact; it does not mean "very good at the current job."

## 💡 Self-reflection Prompts

1. What decision or workflow did my analytics change, and how do I know?
2. Where did I clarify a metric, assumption, or data-quality issue before it damaged trust?
3. What analytical asset did I make reusable or easier for others to own?
4. How did I make another analyst or stakeholder more autonomous?
5. How did AI measurably accelerate my work while preserving correctness and traceability?

Seniority is demonstrated through sustained evidence. No single technology, project, certification, or isolated achievement defines the level.
