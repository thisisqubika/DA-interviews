# 📊 DAS2 — Data Analyst Senior II

*Analytics reference and decision-intelligence advisor*

You own analytics domains and decision systems, not only dashboards. You align metrics, semantic models, stakeholder workflows, analytics products, and adoption to measurable outcomes while developing other analysts and strengthening Qubika's analytics capability.

> Source: [DAS2 - Data Analyst Senior II (version 2026)](https://qubika.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/QCPF/pages/5421006872/DAS2+-+Data+Analyst+Senior+II+version+2026) — Qubika's Career Path Framework (QCPF), last modified Jul 28, 2026.

## 👤 Role Identity

- Owns analytics roadmaps, KPI frameworks, semantic models, and decision-support outcomes across a substantial domain.
- Acts as a trusted advisor to business and data leaders, combining analytical depth with strong facilitation and commercial awareness.
- Scales delivery through analysts, standards, reusable assets, and clear ownership rather than personal heroics.
- Guides responsible use of conversational and AI-enabled analytics grounded in trusted semantics, security, and evaluation.

## 🌱 Growth Zone

At this level, your value shifts from personal analytics expertise to systemic decision impact. You create shared language, trusted metrics, reusable analytical foundations, stronger analysts, and workflows that help the customer act.

### ▶️ Forward-Deployed Direction

Your forward-deployed direction is an Analytics FDE profile: embed with business and technical teams, anchor work in shared outcomes, build governed analytics and lightweight applications, shorten the path from signal to decision, and enable the customer to sustain the capability.

## 🤝 1. Teamwork

Creates the conditions for multiple people and teams to succeed through trust, alignment, accountability, and distributed ownership across analytics and decision intelligence and adjacent disciplines.

**What is Expected**
- Models collaborative leadership in high-stakes, cross-functional, and cross-cultural settings.
- Distributes ownership deliberately and creates clear decision boundaries, context, and accountability.
- Coaches engineers, analysts, scientists, or guides through technical challenges, interpersonal dynamics, and stakeholder pressure.
- Resolves conflict and cross-team blockers without sacrificing standards, trust, or momentum.
- Protects team health while remaining transparent about commitments, risks, and performance.
- Builds durable collaboration across business, product, delivery, engineering, and partner teams.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- Several people or leads operate more autonomously because of deliberate coaching and delegated ownership.
- Cross-team delivery was recovered or accelerated through effective alignment.
- A conflict, ownership gap, or organizational dependency was resolved with a durable operating agreement.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward broader organizational leadership, developing operating models that scale across the Studio.

## 💬 2. Communication

Creates clarity and drives decisions across executive, business, product, delivery, and technical audiences for complex analytics domains, semantic systems, and decision workflows.

**What is Expected**
- Translates technical and analytical choices into business implications, delivery plans, risks, and decisions.
- Leads discovery, workshops, executive updates, pre-sales discussions, and difficult client conversations.
- Communicates proactively about uncertainty, delivery health, technical risk, cost, adoption, and organizational dependencies.
- Frames recommendations with context, alternatives, trade-offs, evidence, and a clear point of view.
- Makes the commercial meaning of technical work explicit: problem, importance, Qubika's role, outcome, and future opportunity.
- Coaches others to communicate with conciseness, intent, and audience awareness.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- A high-stakes workshop or decision forum produced alignment, ownership, and action.
- Executive or client feedback recognizes credibility, clarity, useful challenge, or trusted-advisor behavior.
- A proposal, roadmap, QBR contribution, architecture or analytics narrative connected technical work to measurable value.

**Growth Direction**
Expand from project and account influence to shaping organizational narratives, strategic partnerships, and senior client relationships.

## 🚀 3. Continuous Improvement

Transforms experience, feedback, and industry change into stronger analytics and decision intelligence systems, capabilities, and organizational learning.

**What is Expected**
- Maintains awareness of relevant platform, AI, governance, product, and engineering developments and evaluates them critically.
- Leads retrospectives, health reviews, or post-incident learning and converts findings into systemic improvement.
- Identifies recurring capability gaps and builds practical enablement plans.
- Guides mentees in defining and achieving meaningful growth plans.
- Sponsors experiments with clear hypotheses, success measures, risk boundaries, and adoption paths.
- Documents and scales lessons through communities of practice, standards, playbooks, or the Center of Excellence.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- A systemic improvement tied to repeated delivery, quality, cost, adoption, or capability outcomes.
- A learning or coaching program with adoption and evidence of improved readiness.
- Lessons from several projects synthesized into a standard or strategic recommendation.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward setting long-term capability direction and developing a broad leadership bench.

## 📦 4. Delivery

Owns analytics domains and decision systems, aligning metrics, semantic models, products, adoption, and stakeholder action to measurable business outcomes.

**What is Expected**
- Leads discovery and prioritization for an analytics domain based on business objectives and shared OKRs.
- Defines or governs KPI frameworks, semantic models, business definitions, ownership, and change processes.
- Shapes an analytics roadmap that balances immediate insight needs with trusted, reusable foundations.
- Leads delivery across analysts and partners with data engineering, product, science, and business teams.
- Integrates analytics into operational workflows, self-service experiences, experimentation, or decision execution where appropriate.
- Measures adoption, decision quality, time to insight, trust, and business impact.
- Recovers at-risk analytics initiatives through clear decisions, scope adjustment, and stakeholder alignment.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- A KPI or semantic framework adopted across teams and used consistently in decisions.
- A portfolio of analytics products with measurable adoption and decision impact.
- A fragmented or low-trust analytics process improved through governance, architecture, and stakeholder alignment.

**Growth Direction**
Expand from domain leadership to account, portfolio, or Studio-level analytics strategy and decision-intelligence capability.

## 👑 5. Leadership

Scales results through other leaders and practitioners, establishes direction, and maintains accountability in ambiguous and high-pressure situations.

**What is Expected**
- Sets clear objectives, priorities, decision principles, ownership, and success measures.
- Builds leadership capacity by delegating significant ownership and coaching others through decisions.
- Provides candid, fair, and actionable feedback, including difficult performance or behavior conversations.
- Makes timely decisions with incomplete information and adapts transparently when evidence changes.
- Creates confidence without hiding uncertainty or risk.
- Connects team execution to client strategy, Qubika priorities, and professional growth.
- Builds succession and reduces key-person dependency.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- Other people successfully own complex work after deliberate coaching and delegation.
- A difficult strategic, delivery, or people decision was handled with clear principles and follow-through.
- The team remains effective without constant dependence on this person.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward organizational leadership that shapes strategy, talent systems, and the operating model of the broader Data organization.

## 🏢 6. Studio / Company Impact

Shapes analytics and decision intelligence capabilities, standards, talent, partnerships, and market credibility beyond a single engagement.

**What is Expected**
- Owns or materially advances a Studio priority such as Databricks partnership growth, AI capability, interviewing, career paths, quality, or reusable accelerators.
- Builds standards, playbooks, architectures, analytical frameworks, or assets adopted across projects.
- Supports proposals, discovery, estimates, staffing, technical due diligence, and strategic client conversations.
- Develops talent through mentoring, performance calibration, technical communities, and meaningful opportunities.
- Represents Qubika credibly in partner, client-leadership, recruiting, and public-facing settings.
- Measures internal initiatives by adoption, capability, efficiency, revenue influence, quality, or strategic outcomes.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- An accelerator, standard, rubric, or operating practice used across projects.
- A partner, proposal, or pre-sales contribution that improved credibility, conversion, or readiness.
- A talent initiative that improved staffing, hiring quality, certifications, promotions, or project performance.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward setting Studio strategy, investment priorities, partnerships, and organizational capability at scale.

## 🔧 7. Technical Expertise / Mastery

Drives analytics architecture and standards across semantic modeling, metrics, BI, self-service, governance, experimentation, and AI-enabled analytics.

**What is Expected**
- Designs governed semantic models, metric layers, analytical data products, and ownership models that scale across teams.
- Drives SQL and BI performance, security, row-level access, lineage, refresh, testing, deployment, and operational support decisions.
- Evaluates BI, semantic, lakehouse, and analytics-engineering choices based on user needs, skills, governance, cost, and operating model.
- Applies strong information design and executive storytelling while preserving traceability to trusted metrics.
- Understands experimentation, segmentation, forecasting, and predictive analytics sufficiently to identify appropriate use and collaborate with data science.
- Designs governed conversational analytics or text-to-SQL experiences, including semantic grounding, evaluation, access control, and failure handling.
- Can lead hands-on work with relevant platforms such as Databricks SQL, Genie, lakehouse semantic patterns, dbt, Power BI, Tableau, or equivalent tools.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- A semantic or KPI architecture adopted across several products or teams.
- A platform, governance, or self-service decision supported by explicit trade-offs and adoption evidence.
- A conversational analytics or decision workflow evaluated for correctness, security, usefulness, latency, and cost.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward enterprise analytics architecture, decision-intelligence offerings, and strategic account or Studio direction.

## 🤖 8. AI Adoption

Scales responsible AI-enabled execution through measurable workflows, governance, coaching, and reusable systems.

**What is Expected**
- Defines and promotes AI-assisted practices tied to delivery, quality, analytical, operational, or business outcomes.
- Builds or sponsors reusable agents, automations, skills, prompts, integrations, or workflows that materially improve team capacity.
- Establishes controls for confidentiality, authorization, human review, testing, traceability, licensing, and output quality.
- Uses evaluation and telemetry where appropriate to assess reliability, adoption, cost, and impact.
- Coaches practitioners on context design, validation, tool selection, and critical judgment.
- Drives governed AI-enabled analytics workflows such as conversational BI, text-to-SQL, narrative generation, metric documentation, or decision support, with semantic grounding and systematic evaluation.
- Identifies where AI should augment people, where deterministic automation is preferable, and where AI creates unacceptable risk.

**What Good Evidence Looks Like**
- An adopted AI-enabled workflow with measured gains in cycle time, quality, coverage, insight generation, or delivery capacity.
- A governed agent or automation with clear ownership, evaluation, access controls, monitoring, and maintenance.
- Evidence that several people or teams became more effective through the capability.

**Growth Direction**
Progress toward organizational AI strategy, investment direction, governance, and differentiated client capabilities.

## ⭐ Rating Reminder

A **3 - Meets expectations** means consistently performing at this seniority. A 4 or 5 requires sustained evidence of meaningful next-level scope and impact; it does not mean "very good at the current job."

## 💭 Self-Reflection Prompts

1. What analytics domain or decision system did I make more trusted, scalable, and useful?
2. Which client decision, operating process, or investment did I influence with evidence?
3. What semantic, metric, or governance system now works without depending on me personally?
4. Who became a stronger analytics owner because of my coaching and delegation?
5. What governed AI-enabled analytics capability did I scale, and how was it evaluated?

Seniority is demonstrated through sustained evidence. No single technology, project, certification, or isolated achievement defines the level.
